Why you have WEBP files you did not ask for

Google released WEBP in 2010 and spent a decade pushing it, because it is roughly 25–35% smaller than JPG at comparable quality. Sites adopted it for the bandwidth savings, so right-click-saving an image from a modern website usually hands you a .webp. Browsers all display it. Plenty of desktop applications, print shops, and document systems still do not accept it, which is where converting to PDF becomes the shortest route to something universally openable.

What the conversion does

  • Each WEBP becomes one page, scaled to fit A4 with the aspect ratio preserved.
  • Batches are fine. Add as many files as you like; they become a single multi-page PDF.
  • Transparency flattens to white. WEBP supports an alpha channel; PDF pages here do not, so transparent areas land on white.
  • Animated WEBP loses the animation. Only the first frame is used, the same as printing a GIF.

A note on quality

WEBP files from websites are usually already compressed fairly hard, because that was the point of using WEBP. Re-encoding them into a PDF compresses them a second time, and generation loss is cumulative. Use High unless you have a specific reason not to — you are unlikely to save much with the lower settings on an already-small source, and you can lose visible detail.

If PDF is not really what you want

A lot of people searching for a WEBP converter do not need a document at all; they need the picture in a format their software will open. If that is you, WEBP to JPG or WEBP to PNG is the more direct answer, and PNG keeps transparency intact. Convert to PDF when the destination genuinely wants a document: a print shop, an upload form, a filing system, or an email attachment you want to arrive in a fixed order.

Copyright, briefly

Converting a file does not change who owns the image. Most images saved from websites are someone else's work. Converting for your own reading, reference, or printing is one thing; republishing is another. That is between you and the rights holder, but it is worth saying out loud.

Questions people actually ask

Why will nothing open my WEBP files?

WEBP is well supported in browsers but still patchy in desktop software, print shops, and older document systems. Converting to PDF, JPG, or PNG gets you a format that everything accepts.

Are my images uploaded?

No. The conversion happens in this browser tab. Turn off your Wi-Fi after the page loads and it still works.

Does transparency survive?

No. Transparent regions are flattened onto white when the image is placed on the PDF page. If you need to keep the alpha channel, convert to PNG instead.

What about animated WEBP?

Only the first frame makes it into the PDF. A page cannot animate, so the rest of the frames are dropped.

Can I put several WEBP images into one PDF?

Yes. Add as many as you want; each becomes a page, in the order you added them.

Should I convert to PDF or to JPG?

Convert to PDF when the destination wants a document, like a form or a printer. Convert to JPG or PNG when you just need the picture itself to open in other software.
