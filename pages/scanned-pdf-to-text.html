What is a scanned PDF?

When you scan a paper document, your scanner produces an image of each page and bundles those images into a PDF. The file extension is still .pdf, but unlike a "real" PDF (one created from a Word doc, LaTeX, or a browser print), there is no underlying text layer. Search engines, screen readers, and copy-paste all see nothing but pixels.

This is also true of:

  • Photographs of book pages saved as PDF.
  • PDFs generated by some fax-to-PDF services.
  • Old court documents, historical archives, and academic scans.
  • Anything imported into a PDF from a mobile scanning app like Adobe Scan, Scannable, or Microsoft Lens.

How OCR turns the image back into text

Optical character recognition works in three stages:

  1. Binarize and deskew — convert the page to black and white, straighten any tilt.
  2. Segment — find lines, then words, then individual character regions.
  3. Recognize — a neural network classifies each region against a learned model of the alphabet, digits, and punctuation.

The engine running here is Tesseract — the open-source OCR project Google maintained for years. We ship the WebAssembly build (Tesseract.js) so it runs entirely in your browser, with no upload.

Privacy: nothing is uploaded

This is the main reason we built the tool. Most "free PDF OCR" sites are SaaS products that upload your scan to their server, run OCR there, and email you the result. That's fine for a flyer, terrible for a tax return, medical record, or anything personal.

To verify: open DevTools → Network before clicking Extract Text. You'll see the OCR engine and English language data download once (about 14 MB combined, cached after the first run). After that, no request goes out for your PDF.

Accuracy tips

  • Resolution matters more than anything else. A 300 DPI scan beats a 150 DPI scan by a wide margin. If you're scanning fresh, use 300 DPI or higher.
  • Use the 3x render scale option if the PDF was scanned at low resolution — it gives OCR more pixels to work with.
  • Clean the page — remove shadows, fold lines, and finger photos before scanning.
  • Handwriting is not supported. Tesseract is a printed-text model.
  • Two-column layouts sometimes get read across the column gutter, mixing the two columns. Crop the PDF to one column at a time if you hit this.

Frequently asked questions

Will it work on a multi-language document?

This page is English-only. Mixed-language documents will recognize the English correctly and produce garbled output for other scripts. We may add per-language pages later.

What's the page-count limit?

There's no hard limit, but memory pressure on mobile gets real around 100 pages. On a laptop you can comfortably run several hundred.

Does it preserve formatting?

No. You get the raw text in reading order. Line breaks are kept; bold/italic, fonts, and layout are not. Use the searchable-PDF output if you need the original look.

Why does the first page take longer than the rest?

The first call downloads the OCR engine WebAssembly (~10 MB) and English language data (~4 MB). Both are cached after that, so subsequent pages and subsequent PDFs are much faster.
