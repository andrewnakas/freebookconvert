When to use this

  • Screenshots: copy text out of a video frame, a Discord screenshot, a tweet you can't select, a paywalled article.
  • Photos of documents: a printed receipt, a textbook page, a recipe card, a whiteboard.
  • Memes and infographics: pull the text out to translate or quote it elsewhere.
  • Old scans: a JPG of a letter or page where the text is locked inside the image.

How it compares to other tools

Google Lens, iOS Live Text, and Windows Snip & Sketch all do on-device OCR if you have the right device. They're great when you're already in the camera or screenshot UI. This tool is for the cases when:

  • You're on a desktop and want to drag-drop a file.
  • You want the result as a .txt file, not just text on the clipboard.
  • You don't want the image leaving your machine for any reason.
  • You're processing a batch and want to script around it.

Quality & accuracy

Tesseract is one of the most-used OCR engines in the world — same one inside Google Books and a hundred SaaS products. Accuracy on a clear, properly oriented printed page is typically 95-99%. Things that hurt accuracy:

  • Low resolution (under 100 px tall for text)
  • Rotation more than a few degrees
  • Heavy compression artifacts (over-saved JPEGs)
  • Decorative fonts and italic-on-italic
  • Text on top of busy backgrounds

Other formats

If your text lives in a multi-page PDF, use the scanned PDF to text page instead — it rasterizes every page and OCRs them in batch. If your text is in an iPhone HEIC photo, convert it to JPG first with HEIC to JPG, or drop it here directly if your browser supports HEIC decoding natively.

Privacy

The image is read into a canvas locally and the pixel data is handed to the in-browser OCR worker. No upload happens at any stage. Reload the page with Wi-Fi off after the first OCR run and it still works (the engine is cached).

FAQ

Why does the output have line breaks in odd places?

Tesseract preserves the visual line breaks from the source image. If a paragraph wraps across three lines, the output has three lines. Run a find/replace on single newlines to merge them into a paragraph.

Can I OCR a whole folder at once?

Not yet from this page — one image at a time. For batch jobs we recommend the npm package tesseract.js directly.

Does it find the language automatically?

No. This page is hard-coded to English. Other languages produce garbage. Per-language pages are on the roadmap.
