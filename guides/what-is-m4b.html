What Is an M4B File? Audiobook Format Explained

An M4B is an ordinary MPEG-4 audio file with chapters, and an extension that tells players "this is a book, remember my place."

Published 2026-09-23 · All guides

The short answer

An M4B file is an audiobook in the MPEG-4 container, the same container as an .m4a music file or an .mp4 video. The audio inside is almost always AAC. What makes it an audiobook isn't the audio but two things around it: a chapter table, so players can show chapters and jump between them, and the .m4b extension itself, which tells Apple's software (and most audiobook apps) to treat it as a book. That means remembering your position, not shuffling, and filing it under Audiobooks instead of Music.

If you rename an .m4b to .m4a, the audio plays exactly the same in a music player. The bytes are identical; only the behavior of the software changes.

Why audiobooks got their own extension

Music players assume short tracks you'll listen to many times, in any order. Audiobooks are the opposite: one very long recording, heard once, in order, often across dozens of sessions. Apple introduced the M4B extension for audiobooks sold through iTunes so that iPods could handle that difference. They'd bookmark the position, keep the book out of shuffle, and show it in its own menu. The format stuck. Today it's the de facto standard for DRM-free audiobooks: Libro.fm, Downpour, and many publishers who sell direct offer M4B downloads, and audiobook servers such as Audiobookshelf and Plex expect it.

What's inside an M4B

  • An AAC audio track. Spoken word is usually mono at 32 to 64 kbps. A single voice doesn't need stereo, and at 64 kbps AAC is indistinguishable from the studio master for narration. That works out to roughly 29 MB per hour at 64 kbps.
  • Chapter markers. A list of start times and titles. MP4 files can store these in two ways: as a hidden QuickTime text track (what Apple software reads) and as a compact "Nero" chapter list (what many other players read). Good tools write both, which is what ffmpeg does and what this site's converters use.
  • Metadata. Title, author (stored as "artist"), narrator, year, description, and genre, which is usually "Audiobook".
  • Cover art. A JPEG or PNG embedded in the file, typically square.

Everything is in one file. That's the practical win over a folder of MP3s: one thing to copy, one thing to back up, and no track-ordering surprises.

M4B vs MP3 for audiobooks

MP3 is the more compatible format. Every car stereo, USB speaker, and cheap player in a drawer reads it. But a book as MP3s is usually a folder of 20 to 100 files, and most players treat each file as a separate song: position is saved per file (if at all), speed resets, and shuffle turns chapter 12 into chapter 3. MP3 can carry chapter markers inside one file (ID3 "CHAP" frames), but support for them is patchy outside podcast apps.

M4B is better when your player understands it, and on phones today almost every audiobook app does. Keep MP3 for devices that don't. Converting between the two is quick. MP3 to M4B joins files into one chaptered book, and M4B to MP3 splits one back into a file per chapter.

Which players support M4B chapters

  • iPhone and iPad: Apple Books, BookPlayer (free and open source), Prologue (for Plex), and Bound.
  • Android: Smart AudioBook Player, Voice (open source), Listen Audiobook Player, and the Audiobookshelf app.
  • Desktop: VLC (Playback → Chapter), foobar2000, IINA, and Apple Books or Music on a Mac.
  • Self-hosted: Audiobookshelf, Plex (with an audiobook agent), and Jellyfin.

Windows' built-in Media Player plays M4B audio, but it ignores chapters.

M4B vs AAX, and DRM

Audible's downloads use .aax or .aaxc. Structurally these are also MPEG-4 audio files with chapters, but the audio is encrypted, and only Audible's apps (or authorized devices) can play them. An M4B, by contrast, is normally DRM-free. The files Apple Books sells are M4B-style too, but protected with Apple's FairPlay DRM, so they only play in Apple's apps. Tools on this site work only with DRM-free files. They don't decrypt anything.

Getting an M4B onto your phone

  • iPhone: save the file to the Files app (AirDrop, iCloud Drive, or a download), tap it, and share to Books, or open it in BookPlayer. Don't add it through the Music app. It will appear as a song.
  • Android: copy it to a folder such as Audiobooks/Book Title/ and point your audiobook app at the Audiobooks folder. The apps scan subfolders and group them by book.

Common questions

Can I just rename .m4a to .m4b?

Yes, and many players will then treat it as an audiobook. Renaming doesn't add chapters, though. A renamed single-chapter M4A is a one-chapter M4B.

Why is my M4B so much smaller than the MP3s I made it from?

Audiobook encoders use mono, low-bitrate AAC. A 128 kbps stereo MP3 of narration carries twice the data a single voice needs. At 64 kbps mono, you won't hear the difference.

What's the maximum length of an M4B?

There's no practical limit in the format. Books of 40+ hours exist as single M4B files. The limit is the memory of whatever tool builds or edits it.
